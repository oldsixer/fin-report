# 投资论证的构建与维护：Introduction 中英文初稿

## 中文 Introduction

投资研究报告的价值，取决于它能否将分散的信息转化为有依据且边界明确的判断。公司披露、行业事件和市场数据提供事实基础，财务计算连接不同指标，经营与估值假设使预测成为可能，而风险分析说明判断在何种条件下需要调整。已有股权研究系统开始将数据分析、概念解释、投资论点和估值联系起来，并以可执行计算和来源可追溯的写作支持报告生成。[1,2] 在此意义上，本文将研报视为一种受当前证据约束的投资论证：报告不仅需要陈述发生了什么，还需要解释为什么这些信息能够支持特定判断，以及这一判断的适用条件。这里的“支持”并不意味着对未来收益的确定性证明，而是要求结论的强度与其事实依据、计算过程和显式假设相匹配。

大语言模型与智能体技术已经推动自动研报生成从信息汇总走向更复杂的分析流程。FinRobot 通过 Data–Concept–Thesis 的分层分析组织股权研究与估值；FinSight 将数据和工具整合进可编程变量空间，并通过 Chain-of-Analysis 和两阶段写作连接分析与报告表达。[1,2] Cogito 使用动态思维图支持研究过程中的自适应探索与多智能体协作；FinRpt 则结合专门数据集、监督微调和强化学习训练研报生成智能体。[3,4] 在证据组织层面，FinKario 通过属性与事件信息构建金融知识图谱，并研究面向演化金融信息的图检索。[5] 这些工作表明，分层分析、工具计算、结构化记忆、图式探索与训练优化均已有实质进展。因此，研究问题不能建立在“现有系统只会拼接文本”或“尚未使用图进行金融分析”这样的笼统前提之上。

与生成方法同步发展的评测研究，也已超出对文本流畅性和最终答案的单一评价。FActScore 将长文本分解为原子事实，衡量其获得可靠来源支持的比例；ALCE 分别评价回答质量与引用质量，为检查生成内容的事实依据提供了基础。[6,7] 更接近研报分析结构的 FinResearchBench 将报告解析为论点与证据构成的逻辑树，并结合支持关系检查和树结构指标评价分析质量。[8] LiveDRBench 使用包含嵌套子主张的中间表示，使上层主张的评价与支撑它的子主张相关联。[9] 这些进展已经说明，论证结构和中间依赖并非现有研究完全忽视的对象。不过，原子事实有据可查、引用能够支持某个句子，以及一组前提足以支撑一个有条件的投资判断，仍然是不同层次的要求。

细粒度基准为这种区分提供了经验动机。FinDeepResearch 在其测试的 16 种方法中发现，7 种方法能够完全遵循指定格式，但最优方法在细粒度内容评价上的得分仅为 37.9/100；其分项分析还显示，战略解释等能力比结构遵循更具挑战。[10] 这里的 37.9 是该基准的评分结果，而不是所有生成事实的真实准确率。Deep FinResearch Bench 则从专业股权研究出发，分别检查报告分析、预测和引用支持，并在所测系统中观察到假设论证、行业特定分析及情景分析方面的不足。[11] 在非金融的复杂研究任务中，DeepResearch Bench II 区分信息召回、分析和呈现，ResearchRubrics 则识别出隐含推理与信息综合相关的失败。[12,13] 这些研究共同提示，内容组织成熟并不能替代对分析依据的检查；但不同任务、模型和评分规则下的结果，不能合并成一个“研报普遍错误率”，也不能单独证明所有缺陷都源于同一种推理机制。

这一判断还需要与已有的积极结果相容。FinResearchBench 的部分模型在其逻辑一致性和分析深度评分上已接近专家报告，而 FinReasoning 所评模型在语义一致性、数据对齐和深层分析任务上呈现不同的能力分布。[8,14] FinReportBench 进一步关注机构级报告的完整性，并通过失败反馈驱动的 skill 优化改善报告生成。[15] 因而，更有针对性的研究方向是识别特定支持要求在何种条件下尚未被满足，而不是否定模型的一般分析能力。仅依据这些总体或分项得分，还不能判定：一个投资判断究竟缺失了哪项关键前提，该前提是否适用于当前主体与期间，以及修复局部分析之后，报告其他部分是否仍与之相容。FinReasoning 明确将局部错误如何传播至下游分析列为其尚未测量的问题。[14]

一个说明性案例可以刻画这一差别。假设资料分别表明，某晶圆厂计划增加资本开支，某公司从事半导体设备业务。两个陈述即使都正确，也不足以直接推出该公司下一年度利润将显著增长。分析还需要处理新增开支与设备品类是否匹配、客户认证与订单获取是否成立、交付验收是否落在预测期内，以及收入增量如何在毛利和费用假设下转化为利润。若进一步提出投资吸引力判断，还需要价格、估值和风险方面的支持。这个案例并非对某一系统的实测失败报告，而是用于界定本文的研究对象：**事实与判断之间的支持条件可能缺失、不适用，或在不同分析环节中被不一致地使用。** 对未来事项，系统可以作出有依据的条件性推断，也可以明确未知并限制结论强度；不能将未经处理的桥接假设默认为已经得到证实。

当证据变化时，同一问题会表现为论证维护的困难。仍以该案例为例，如果新披露表明订单验收推迟，系统就需要重新判断原收入确认条件是否成立，重算受影响的盈利预测，并检查据此形成的业务判断和估值分析。历史财务事实和无关的有效分析不应因此被任意改变；失效的支持路径也不能因为旧结论已经写入报告而被保留。更重要的是，支持发生变化并不规定唯一的修订动作：若存在独立的替代支持，原判断可能继续成立；若关键前提尚不确定，合理修订可能是降低确定性或收缩适用范围，而不是将结论直接反转。因此，需要维护的是判断与当前支持条件之间的关系，而非单纯保持文本稳定或机械地重写所有关联段落。

证据驱动的文本更新与报告修订已经有直接的研究先例。FRUIT 将新证据条件下的既有文章更新定义为生成任务，WiNELL 通过智能体搜集更新信息，并使用基于人工编辑历史训练的模型生成细粒度修改。[16,17] 更直接相关的 MR DRE 将深度研究报告的多轮修订纳入评测，检查内容、格式与自我反思反馈下的修改表现。其测试发现，智能体在处理反馈时仍会破坏原有内容覆盖，并可能降低引用质量，且所测试的提示修改和专门修订子智能体未能充分解决问题。[18] 这项工作已将“完成新修改的同时保留原有质量”确立为明确的研究对象。但其核心设置是用户反馈驱动的报告修订，不能直接替代对金融前提变更、财务重算与投资判断调整之间依赖关系的检验。

知识编辑与检索增强生成研究为分析这种依赖提供了另一组基础。MQuAKE 检查事实更新是否正确影响依赖这些事实的多跳回答；RippleEdits 同时考察更新后的逻辑泛化、一致性和无关知识保留。[19,20] EchoEdit 进一步通过 RippleGraph、传播规则和蒸馏的推理规划器处理多跳知识更新。[21] CUE-R 则对检索证据执行删除、替换和重复操作，观察答案质量与可见证据使用行为的变化。[22] 因此，事实变更测试、依赖传播和选择性保留均已有方法依据。将这些思想用于投资研报，仍需处理一个不同的目标对象：在本文关注的论证中，事实、可执行计算、尚未实现的经营假设和可撤销判断共同构成支持关系，某项前提变化后的合理结果未必是一个确定答案的替换。

综合这些研究，本文关注的缺口可以更具体地表述为：如何将初始论证中的支持条件建立，与证据变化后的支持条件重检，置于同一个可检验的问题框架之中。现有文献分别提供了金融分析生成、结构化支持评价、报告修订以及知识更新的机制与基准，但这些结果尚不足以回答，同一系统能否为关键投资判断建立适用的前提组合，并在这些前提变化后，协调完成必要的计算与判断修订，同时保留未受影响且仍然有效的内容。[2,8,14,18–22] 这一定位并不主张已有方法必然失败，也不把应用图结构本身视为贡献。经典真值维护系统早已通过记录判断的理由及其依赖关系，在假设变化时调整信念，为这种问题表述提供了理论先例。[23] 面向自然语言研报，仍需解决的关键是如何从不完全资料中识别适用支持、显式处理假设与未知，并将支持状态变化落实为可核查的报告修订。

初始生成与后续修订因而可以共享一种要求：当前判断应与当前证据、适用条件和分析依赖相容。但二者并不等价。生成需要发现尚未建立的支持，修订还需识别哪些既有支持已经失效，并防止将旧错误当作应当保留的内容；能够维持内部一致，也不意味着报告已充分回答研究问题。同时，支持不足可能来自资料缺失，也可能来自系统未能正确利用已有资料。Sufficient Context 对检索上下文充分性与模型使用能力的区分，为分离这两类问题提供了依据，但不能直接替代金融论证的领域检验。[24] 因此，本文将论证的构建与维护作为共同研究对象，而不预先假定它们具有相同的失效原因，或必然可以由同一种算法同时解决。由此形成本文的核心研究问题：

> **在给定证据与明确假设的约束下，如何构建并维护投资论证，使关键判断获得充分且适用的前提支持；并在证据变化时，选择性地修订受影响的计算、业务判断与投资结论，保留未受影响且仍然有效的内容，恢复论证整体的一致性？**

## English Introduction

The value of an investment research report depends on its ability to turn dispersed information into justified judgments with explicit boundaries. Corporate disclosures, industry events, and market data establish the factual basis. Financial calculations connect quantities, operating and valuation assumptions enable forecasts, and risk analysis identifies conditions under which a judgment should be reconsidered. Existing equity research systems have begun to connect data analysis, conceptual interpretation, investment theses, and valuation, supported by executable computation and source-grounded writing [1,2]. We therefore view an investment report as an argument constrained by the evidence available at the time: it should explain not only what has happened, but also why the available information supports a particular judgment and under which conditions that judgment applies. Support in this setting is not a deterministic proof of future returns. Rather, the strength of a conclusion should be commensurate with its evidence, calculations, and explicit assumptions.

Large language models and agents have extended automated financial reporting beyond information aggregation. FinRobot organizes equity research and valuation through a Data–Concept–Thesis hierarchy. FinSight integrates data and tools into a programmable variable space, connecting analysis to report writing through Chain-of-Analysis representations and a two-stage writing process [1,2]. Cogito introduces a dynamic graph of thoughts for adaptive exploration and multi-agent collaboration, while FinRpt trains report-generation agents using supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning [3,4]. At the evidence-organization level, FinKario constructs financial knowledge graphs from attribute and event information and develops graph-based retrieval for evolving financial knowledge [5]. These approaches establish substantive precedents for hierarchical analysis, tool-based computation, structured memory, graph-based exploration, and training-based improvement. A research agenda cannot therefore rest on the assumption that existing systems merely concatenate text or have not used graphs for financial analysis.

Evaluation has likewise progressed beyond fluency and final-answer accuracy. FActScore decomposes long-form text into atomic facts and measures their support in reliable sources. ALCE evaluates answer and citation quality, providing a basis for assessing whether generated content is supported by its references [6,7]. Closer to the structure of financial analysis, FinResearchBench parses reports into logic trees of arguments and evidence, combining support checks with structural measures of analytical quality [8]. LiveDRBench uses an intermediate representation with nested subclaims, linking the evaluation of higher-level claims to their supporting components [9]. Argument structure and intermediate dependencies are thus already recognized evaluation targets. Nevertheless, verifying an atomic fact, establishing that a citation supports a sentence, and determining whether a set of premises adequately supports a conditional investment judgment remain distinct requirements.

Fine-grained benchmarks provide empirical motivation for this distinction. Among the 16 methods evaluated in FinDeepResearch, seven fully complied with the prescribed format, whereas the highest fine-grained content score was 37.9 out of 100. Its component analysis also identified greater difficulty with strategic interpretation than with structural compliance [10]. This score is specific to the benchmark's rubric, not an estimate of the proportion of all generated facts that are true. Deep FinResearch Bench separately assesses professional investment analysis, forecasting, and citation support, identifying limitations in assumption justification, sector-specific analysis, and scenario analysis in the evaluated systems [11]. In broader research tasks, DeepResearch Bench II distinguishes information recall, analysis, and presentation, while ResearchRubrics identifies failures involving implicit reasoning and information synthesis [12,13]. Together, these findings motivate examining analytical support separately from polished organization. They do not establish a universal error rate for investment reports or show that every observed deficiency has the same underlying cause.

This interpretation must also accommodate positive findings. On some logic-consistency and analysis-depth measures in FinResearchBench, model-generated reports score close to expert-written reports. FinReasoning, meanwhile, reveals different capability profiles across semantic consistency, data alignment, and deep analytical insight [8,14]. FinReportBench addresses institution-grade completeness and improves report generation through failure-grounded skill optimization [15]. The resulting research opportunity is to identify specific support requirements that remain unmet under identifiable conditions, rather than to deny models' general analytical capabilities. These aggregate or component scores alone do not reveal which prerequisite a particular investment judgment lacks, whether that prerequisite applies to the relevant entity and period, or whether a local correction leaves the rest of the report compatible with the revised analysis. FinReasoning explicitly identifies the propagation of local errors into downstream analysis as an unmeasured problem [14].

An illustrative case clarifies the target. Suppose the available materials state that a semiconductor foundry plans to increase capital expenditure and that a particular company supplies semiconductor equipment. Even if both statements are correct, they do not by themselves justify a forecast of substantial profit growth for that company in the following year. The argument must address product compatibility, customer qualification and order acquisition, the timing of delivery and acceptance, and the conversion of incremental revenue into profit under margin and expense assumptions. A further claim of investment attractiveness also requires support concerning price, valuation, and risk. This is a hypothetical example, not an observed failure attributed to a particular system. It identifies the problem of interest: support conditions linking facts to judgments may be missing, inapplicable, or used inconsistently across analytical stages. For uncertain future events, a system may offer justified conditional reasoning or acknowledge unknowns and qualify its conclusion. It should not silently treat unresolved bridging assumptions as established facts.

Changing evidence exposes the maintenance aspect of the same problem. If a subsequent disclosure indicates delayed order acceptance, the system must reconsider the revenue-recognition condition, recompute affected earnings forecasts, and inspect the business judgments and valuation analysis that depend on them. Historical financial facts and unrelated valid analysis should not change arbitrarily. Conversely, an invalidated support path should not survive simply because its conclusion appeared in the previous report. A change in support does not prescribe a unique revision: independent alternative support may preserve a judgment, while uncertainty about a necessary premise may warrant reduced confidence or a narrower scope rather than the opposite conclusion. The object to maintain is the relationship between judgments and their current support conditions, not textual stability alone or indiscriminate rewriting of connected passages.

Evidence-grounded text updating and report revision already have direct precedents. FRUIT formulates the task of updating an existing article given new evidence. WiNELL combines agent-based information gathering with fine-grained editing models trained on human revision histories [16,17]. More directly, MR DRE evaluates multi-turn revision of deep research reports under content, format, and self-reflection feedback. Its experiments identify regressions in previously covered content and citation quality while agents address feedback, which the tested prompt modifications and dedicated revision sub-agent do not adequately resolve [18]. Preserving existing quality while implementing requested changes is therefore an established research problem. However, user-feedback-driven revision does not directly test the dependencies connecting changes in financial premises to recalculation and adjustments in investment judgments.

Knowledge editing and retrieval-augmented generation provide complementary foundations. MQuAKE tests whether updated facts correctly affect dependent multi-hop answers, and RippleEdits examines logical generalization, consistency, and the preservation of unrelated knowledge after an edit [19,20]. EchoEdit introduces a RippleGraph, propagation rules, and a distilled reasoning planner for multi-hop knowledge updates [21]. CUE-R removes, replaces, or duplicates retrieved evidence and measures changes in answer quality and observable evidence-use behavior [22]. Fact-change tests, dependency propagation, and selective preservation thus have established methodological precedents. Investment arguments nevertheless present a different target: factual evidence, executable calculations, unrealized operating assumptions, and defeasible judgments jointly constitute support, and a premise change need not entail replacing one determinate answer with another.

These research streams motivate a more specific question: how can establishing support conditions during initial argument construction and rechecking them after evidence changes be studied within one testable problem formulation? Existing work supplies mechanisms and benchmarks for financial analysis generation, structured support evaluation, report revision, and knowledge updating. Its findings do not yet resolve whether a single system can establish applicable combinations of premises for key investment judgments and, when those premises change, coordinate the necessary revisions to calculations and judgments while preserving unaffected content that remains valid [2,8,14,18–22]. This framing neither assumes that existing methods must fail nor treats the use of a graph as a contribution in itself. Classical truth maintenance systems already record justifications and dependencies to revise beliefs as assumptions change [23]. For natural-language investment reports, the outstanding challenge is to identify applicable support from incomplete materials, represent assumptions and unknowns explicitly, and translate changes in support status into revisions that can be checked.

Initial generation and subsequent revision can therefore share a requirement: current judgments should remain compatible with current evidence, applicability conditions, and analytical dependencies. They are not equivalent tasks. Generation must discover support that has not yet been established. Revision must additionally identify invalidated support without mistaking existing errors for content worth preserving. Internal consistency alone also does not ensure that a report adequately addresses its research question. Moreover, insufficient support can arise either from missing information or from failure to use the information already available. The distinction between context sufficiency and model utilization developed in Sufficient Context provides a basis for separating these possibilities, but does not replace domain-specific investigation of investment arguments [24]. We consequently treat argument construction and maintenance as a shared research object without presupposing identical failure mechanisms or a single algorithm that necessarily solves both. This leads to our central research question:

> **How can an investment argument be constructed and maintained under given evidence and explicit assumptions, so that its key judgments have sufficient and applicable premise support and, when evidence changes, affected calculations, business judgments, and investment conclusions are selectively revised, unaffected content that remains valid is preserved, and consistency across the argument is restored?**
